## 2. What VoiceWink and third parties process — and where

**At a glance — what leaves your device.** VoiceWink is local-first: by default almost nothing leaves your device, and the app never sends your audio or transcripts to DV Studio. (What you choose to attach to a support email yourself is the one exception — see Section 7.)

Where a third party processes data for its own purposes — such as Lemon Squeezy's checkout and billing as merchant of record, or a cloud provider you enable under your own account and API key — that processing is carried out under the responsibility of that third party, not DV Studio (see Section 3). For the processing this policy attributes to DV Studio — the license-validation calls, opt-in crash reporting, the update check, the one-time Microsoft Store install reports, the one-time trial-start and website installer reports and the recording of downloads of the versioned installer files, the model download, support correspondence, and the websites — DV Studio is the controller (see Sections 3 to 7).

The only data that leaves your device is:

- **License key + activation identifiers** (a random device label on activation; the instance identifier issued by Lemon Squeezy on re-validation and deactivation — no hostname or personal identifier) — to Lemon Squeezy, automatically, to keep your activation valid. See §3 (Category A).
- **IP address, app version, update channel** — to Cloudflare, on the automatic update check, which you can turn off. See §4.
- **One-time Microsoft Store install reports** (each one request to updates.voicewink.app carrying your IP address, a User-Agent naming VoiceWink, and, as part of the address, the update channel and which report it is — no version and no installation or device identifier) — to Cloudflare, from copies built for the Microsoft Store's own update channel: normally one from the installer while it installs VoiceWink, and one after an automatic update check, which is never sent while automatic update checking is off. Earlier versions on the general public channel send only the second, and only when they find a Microsoft Store installation event. See §4.5, which also says when a report can repeat.
- **A one-time trial-start report and a one-time website installer report** (each one request to updates.voicewink.app carrying your IP address, a User-Agent naming VoiceWink, and, as part of the address, the update channel and which report it is — no version and no installation or device identifier) — to Cloudflare: the first once after you start the free trial, after an automatic update check, which is never sent while automatic update checking is off; the second from the installer offered on voicewink.app while it installs VoiceWink. See §4.6.
- **A model file request** (the requested file's name — never audio, transcripts, text, or keys) — to DV Studio's model-download endpoint models.voicewink.app, operated on our behalf by Cloudflare (for a speech model only, and only if that endpoint cannot deliver the file correctly, to Hugging Face as fallback), when a local speech model you selected is not yet on your device (for example on first use) or when you download a local AI-enhancement model. See §3 (Category A) for the Cloudflare-operated endpoint, and the model-download paragraph below.
- **Crash diagnostics** (never transcripts, keys, or your hostname) — to Sentry, only if you opt in to crash reporting. See §3 and §8.
- **Support emails and anything you attach** — to DV Studio, when you email support. See §7.
- **Audio or text for a cloud feature, plus your API key** (to authenticate), and — for some transcription providers — **your Dictionary and trigger words** — to a provider you choose at your own risk and under your own responsibility, not DV Studio, only when you enable that BYOK feature. See §3 (Category B).

Everything not in this list — your recordings, transcripts, settings, dictionary, API keys, reference images, and generated images — stays on your device under %LOCALAPPDATA%/VoiceWink.

VoiceWink runs locally on your Windows device. The categories of data it handles are:

- **Audio captured during recording.** Held transiently in RAM and written to a temporary WAV file on your local disk, which is deleted automatically once the recording has been transcribed. Three situations keep it for longer, all of them on your own device:
  - **A transcription that fails.** The recording is kept so that you can retry it, and is removed when you retry, start a new recording, or close the app. A 7-day sweep is the backstop.
  - **A recording in which VoiceWink detects no speech.** Transcription is not attempted, and the recording is kept in the same way, so that you can still retry it if the detection was wrong.
  - **The keep-recordings debugging option** (off by default). Each recording is moved to a local `Recordings\Debug` folder at the point where it would otherwise be deleted, and is removed automatically after 7 days.

  In all three cases the files stay under %LOCALAPPDATA%/VoiceWink and are never uploaded by DV Studio.
- **Microphone access while the app is running.** With the "Instant recording" setting on (the default), VoiceWink keeps a microphone capture open the whole time the app runs, so that pressing the record hotkey starts capturing immediately rather than after the device spins up. That continuous capture feeds a **memory-only buffer holding at most about the last 10 seconds of audio**, which is continuously overwritten, never written to disk, and never transmitted. Audio from the moment you press the hotkey onward is written to the recording file; the cut is approximate, so a recording can include a brief moment from just before the press (never more than the buffer holds). Everything else is discarded in memory within seconds. Windows shows its microphone-in-use indicator for as long as the app runs, and turning the setting off releases the microphone between recordings.
- **Transcribed text.** Written to your local SQLite database under %LOCALAPPDATA%/VoiceWink, only if history is enabled.
- **Settings and dictionary entries.** Stored locally in JSON and SQLite under %LOCALAPPDATA%/VoiceWink.
- **API keys and license keys.** Stored locally and encrypted via the Windows Data Protection API (DPAPI) so they can only be read by your Windows user account on this device. (When you use a BYOK cloud feature, the relevant provider key is also sent to that provider — and only that provider — to authenticate the request; see Section 3 Category B.)
- **Generated images.** If you use the image-generation feature, the images returned by the provider you selected are written to your local disk under %LOCALAPPDATA%/VoiceWink. They are not sent anywhere by DV Studio.
- **Reference images you attach to an image generation.** When History is enabled and you attach one or more reference images — your own files, or images VoiceWink generated earlier — a **local copy of each** is stored under %LOCALAPPDATA%/VoiceWink so that regenerating still works after you restart the app or move the original. A copy is **kept for as long as any History entry references it** and is erased once the last such entry is deleted; where a regeneration is still using a copy at that moment, erasure is deferred until that use finishes. With History disabled, no copy is kept. "Delete all my data" removes the whole folder immediately. The copies are local only — a reference image leaves your device solely as part of an image-generation request you explicitly trigger, which carries the image regardless of whether a copy is retained.
- **Crash reports (opt-in).** Sent to Sentry only if you have explicitly enabled crash reporting. Stack traces, app version, OS version, your graphics adapter models and display driver versions, and redacted log breadcrumbs are sent. Transcriptions, API keys, license keys, and your machine hostname are never sent.
- **Problem-report bundles and diagnostic logs.** A report you build with "Report a problem" is written to a local `Reports` folder and removed after 7 days if it is never sent; it is covered by the in-app "Delete all data" control and by the GDPR export's media option. If you switch on the optional prompt-trace logging (off by default), the dictated text and AI prompts are additionally written to local log files, which are swept after 7 days. See Section 7 for what happens when you choose to attach any of this to a support email.

Local data does not leave your device unless you enable a third-party cloud feature at your own risk and under your own responsibility — except for five automatic network calls: license validation to Lemon Squeezy (Section 3 Category A, required to keep your activation valid), the update check to Cloudflare (Section 4, which you can turn off at any time in the app), the one-time Microsoft Store install reports to Cloudflare (Section 4.5, sent by the copies described there; the one that follows an automatic update check is off whenever that check is off), the one-time trial-start and website installer reports to Cloudflare (Section 4.6; the trial-start report is off whenever automatic update checking is off), and the model download from models.voicewink.app, also served by Cloudflare, which happens only when a speech model you selected is not yet on your device or when you download an AI-enhancement model (for a speech model only, and only if that endpoint cannot deliver the file correctly, the download is retried from Hugging Face — see the next paragraph).

**Local model downloads.** Local transcription uses locally stored speech-model files, and VoiceWink's built-in local AI enhancement uses an AI-enhancement model downloaded to your device. When the speech model you selected in the app is not yet on your device, or when you download an AI-enhancement model, VoiceWink downloads the model's files over an encrypted connection from **models.voicewink.app**, DV Studio's model-download endpoint. **Cloudflare** hosts those files on its R2 object storage and serves them via its global edge network on DV Studio's behalf (Section 3, Category A — the same processor that operates the update endpoint in Section 4). Each request discloses your IP address, standard HTTP request metadata, and the name of the requested file — never audio, transcripts, text, or keys. AI-enhancement model files are downloaded only from models.voicewink.app; such a model runs on your device, and the text it processes does not leave your device. Only for a speech model, and only if models.voicewink.app cannot deliver the file correctly — it is unreachable, or what it serves fails the integrity check — does VoiceWink automatically retry the download from **Hugging Face** (huggingface.co), the public repository the files are mirrored from; such a fallback request discloses the same data categories, and Hugging Face processes it under its own privacy policy, for which DV Studio bears no responsibility whatsoever. The downloaded files are stored locally under %LOCALAPPDATA%/VoiceWink/Models and each file's integrity is verified after download.

**Special-category data.** VoiceWink does not use your voice to identify you — it transcribes speech to text and creates no voiceprint — so it does not process biometric data for the purpose of identification under Article 9 GDPR. We do not intentionally collect special-category data (such as data revealing health, religion, or political opinions). Because you control what you dictate, your audio or transcripts could incidentally contain such content; that content is processed locally on your device, or — if you enable a cloud feature — sent only to the third-party provider you selected under your own account, and DV Studio neither receives nor stores it. The selected third-party provider processes personal data under its own privacy policy for which DV Studio bears no responsibility whatsoever.

**Data from third parties.** The one third-party source we receive personal data from is **Lemon Squeezy**: when you buy or activate a key, Lemon Squeezy makes the order and license data available to DV Studio (buyer name, email address, country, order history, and issued license keys — card and payment data stay with Lemon Squeezy and Stripe; see Sections 3 and 5). Beyond that, the only data we hold is what your device sends as described in this policy, or what you send us directly (for example, by email).

## 4. Automatic update check

VoiceWink contacts updates.voicewink.app to check whether a newer version of the Software is available. By default this check fires shortly after the application starts and then repeats approximately every 24 hours while the application is running. When the automatic-installation setting is on (the default), an update found by the check may be downloaded and installed automatically; the download reveals the same data categories as the check itself (IP address, User-Agent, channel, version — nothing new). You can turn off automatic update checking and automatic update installation at any time in the app.

### 4.1 What is sent

Each update check is a single HTTPS GET to `https://updates.voicewink.app/<channel>/releases.<channel>.json`. The request carries:

- Your **IP address** (visible to Cloudflare as the operator of the endpoint, as for any HTTPS request).
- The **HTTP User-Agent** identifying the Velopack update library and its version (e.g. Velopack/0.0.1298).
- The **release channel** the Software is built against (e.g. win-x64-stable), included as part of the request URL.
- The **currently-installed version** of the Software, included as a query string or as part of the Velopack library's request shape (used by the Software itself to decide whether the manifest reports an upgrade — it is not separately persisted by DV Studio).

No transcribed text, audio, API keys, license keys, or local identifiers other than the four items above are sent. (The separate one-time Microsoft Store install reports are described in 4.5, and the one-time trial-start and website installer reports in 4.6.)

### 4.2 Who receives it

The endpoint is served by **Cloudflare** from a Cloudflare R2 bucket controlled by DV Studio. Cloudflare's default request logs (which may include source IP, timestamp, request path, and basic HTTP metadata) are governed by Cloudflare's privacy policy and retained per Cloudflare's defaults. The manifest is a static object served by R2, and DV Studio does **not** log individual update checks. From Cloudflare's aggregated request statistics for this endpoint, DV Studio counts per day and per hour how many update checks, installer downloads and install reports from earlier versions (4.5) it received, and keeps those daily and hourly totals, which hold no IP address or other request detail, for as long as it distributes the Software.

In addition, three kinds of request to this endpoint are recorded one by one: downloads of the versioned installer files (`VoiceWinkApp-<version>-<channel>-Setup.exe`), the form in which the Microsoft Store listing refers to the installer (the website links a different, unversioned file, which is not recorded this way), the install reports sent by copies built for the Microsoft Store's own update channel (4.5), and the trial-start and website installer reports (4.6). Reports from earlier versions are not recorded this way. For each such request, a program DV Studio runs on Cloudflare's network (a Cloudflare Worker) records one entry holding: the time; for a download, the requested file name, which states the version and update channel, and for a report, which report it is and the update channel; the approximate country, and whether Cloudflare attributes the request's network to Microsoft, both as Cloudflare derives them from the IP address; and, for a download, the byte range requested, the response status and the size of the response. A report is recorded only when its User-Agent names VoiceWink; the User-Agent is read for that purpose only. The IP address and the User-Agent are not recorded, and the program keeps no log of its own, so DV Studio cannot tell which entries concern you from the data it keeps, and cannot apply an access or erasure request to them unless you provide information that identifies them (Article 11 GDPR). These entries describe traffic: with no identifier linking one request to another, they do not establish how many complete downloads or successful installations occurred. The network flag separates traffic from networks operated by Microsoft, which checks each Microsoft Store submission, from other traffic; either group can include people's own downloads and automated requests. The entries are kept in Workers Analytics Engine on Cloudflare's systems for about three months; DV Studio keeps the daily totals derived from them, with no request-level detail, for as long as it distributes the Software. For the trial-start and website installer reports (4.6) it also keeps hourly totals, likewise with no request-level detail and for as long as it distributes the Software.

### 4.3 Legal basis

Update checking relies on **Article 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR** — performance of the contract, because the updates it finds are included with your activation key (see the EULA, section 12.2), and **legitimate interests**, balancing your interest in receiving security and bug-fix updates against the minimal data collected. The data minimization is structural: no app-level analytics other than the one-time reports in 4.5 and 4.6, no separately-retained logs of update checks, and the user-facing automatic-update-check setting is honored before the network call is made.

### 4.4 Your control

The app provides a setting that turns automatic update checking off; when it is off, the automatic check never fires. That choice is offered during first-run setup, and both automatic update checking and automatic update installation can be turned off at any time. A manual check remains available and works as a one-shot request — using it is itself a deliberate, user-initiated request for that single check.

### 4.5 One-time Microsoft Store install reports

So that DV Studio can count installations made through the Microsoft Store, copies of VoiceWink built for the Microsoft Store's own update channel (win-x64-store-stable) send two short reports. Such a copy identifies its channel from its own configuration, without reading anything else on your device. Its installer can also be downloaded and run directly, so a report shows the channel a copy was built for, not that the Microsoft Store installed it. Copies on any other update channel send no new reports of the two kinds this section describes; earlier versions on the general public channel are the exception described under "Earlier versions" below.

**What is sent.** Each report is a single HTTPS GET to updates.voicewink.app. It carries your IP address (visible to Cloudflare as the operator of the endpoint, as for any HTTPS request), a User-Agent naming VoiceWink, and, as part of the address, the update channel and which report it is — and nothing else: no version, no license key, no installation or device identifier. There are two reports:

- **The installer's report**, to `https://updates.voicewink.app/<channel>/install-source/msstore/installer`, which the installer attempts once while it installs VoiceWink — after VoiceWink's files have been placed on your device and before the installation has finished, so it does not confirm that the installation succeeded. It is attempted before VoiceWink first starts after that installation, and therefore before first-run setup offers you the choice of automatic update checking. It is not attempted when the installer finds settings left on your device by an earlier installation in which automatic update checking is turned off, or settings it cannot read. The installer waits at most five seconds for it and never retries it; the only trace it keeps is one line in its local installation log stating the outcome (an HTTP status, an error type, or why it was not sent — never an address). A failure to send it does not affect the installation. Each installation, a reinstallation included, attempts it once.
- **The first-check report**, to `https://updates.voicewink.app/<channel>/install-source/msstore`, sent after an automatic update check succeeds, starting with the first such check for this installation. VoiceWink keeps in your local settings, against the installation folder's creation time, whether this report has been delivered; it stops once it has received any HTTP response and saved that result, and makes at most three attempts for each installation record it keeps. An interrupted delivery or a failure to save its result can produce duplicate reports, and deleting VoiceWink's local settings can allow further reports.

**Earlier versions.** Copies of earlier versions on the general public channel (win-x64-stable) that include the first-check report — however they were installed — send only that report, to that channel's address, and decide whether to send it from Windows' Microsoft Store event log: at each automatic update check until a result for this installation is saved, they read the Microsoft Store installation events that Windows recorded during a limited period around the time the VoiceWink installation folder was created, and send the report only if they find the event recording that VoiceWink's Microsoft Store product finished installing. Those events can concern other products; they are inspected on your device only to look for that event, and are neither kept nor sent. The result is kept in your local settings. It can be wrong (for example, when VoiceWink was installed from the Microsoft Store and reinstalled another way shortly afterwards) or unavailable (for example, when Windows no longer holds the event). A copy of a later version on the general public channel does not read the event log or decide anything new, but completes the delivery of a report that an earlier version of the same installation had decided to send.

**How it is used.** The reports from copies built for the Microsoft Store's own update channel are recorded as described in Section 4.2; the reports from earlier versions are counted only in the daily and hourly totals described there. DV Studio counts the reports received each day — reports received, not installations completed that day — and counts those from networks operated by Microsoft separately from the others.

**Legal basis and your control.** This relies on DV Studio's legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) in knowing how many copies of the Software are installed through the Microsoft Store, balanced against the limited data involved: one fact about the copy, normally reported once by the installer and once after the first successful automatic update check, with no installation or device identifier. The first-check report follows the automatic update-check setting (4.4): while automatic update checking is off, nothing is read and it is not sent, and a manual update check never sends it. The installer's report is attempted before that setting can be chosen, except as described above; if you do not want it sent, install VoiceWink from voicewink.app rather than from the Microsoft Store (that installer sends the separate website installer report described in 4.6 instead).

### 4.6 One-time trial-start and website installer reports

So that DV Studio can count how many free trials start and how many copies are installed with the installer offered on voicewink.app, VoiceWink sends two further short reports.

**What is sent.** Each report is a single HTTPS GET to updates.voicewink.app. It carries your IP address (visible to Cloudflare as the operator of the endpoint, as for any HTTPS request), a User-Agent naming VoiceWink, and, as part of the address, the update channel and which report it is — and nothing else: no version, no license key, no installation or device identifier. There are two reports:

- **The trial-start report**, to `https://updates.voicewink.app/report/<channel>/trial-start`. When the free trial starts on your device, VoiceWink notes in your local settings that this report is due, and sends it after the next automatic update check that reaches the update endpoint: normally within a minute of finishing first-run setup, otherwise shortly after VoiceWink next starts or up to about 24 hours later while it keeps running. VoiceWink records the report as sent before making the request and never retries it, so it is sent at most once for each trial start, and a failed request is not repeated. Continuing a trial that already exists on your device, for example after a reinstallation, does not make a new report due; a report that was already due and is still in your local settings can still be sent. Deleting VoiceWink's local settings before the report is sent cancels it.
- **The website installer's report**, to `https://updates.voicewink.app/report/<channel>/installer`, which the installer of copies built for the general public update channel (win-x64-stable) — the installer that voicewink.app offers — attempts once while it installs VoiceWink. It follows the same rules as the Microsoft Store installer's report in 4.5: it is attempted after VoiceWink's files have been placed on your device and before the installation has finished, so it does not confirm that the installation succeeded, and before VoiceWink first starts, and therefore before first-run setup offers you the choice of automatic update checking; it is not attempted when the installer finds settings left on your device by an earlier installation in which automatic update checking is turned off, or settings it cannot read; the installer waits at most five seconds for it and never retries it; the only trace it keeps is one line in its local installation log stating the outcome (an HTTP status, an error type, or why it was not sent — never an address); and a failure to send it does not affect the installation. Each installation, a reinstallation included, attempts it once; an update of VoiceWink does not. The same installer obtained from anywhere else sends it too, so the report shows the channel a copy was built for, not where its installer was downloaded.

## 7. Retention

Local data (transcripts, settings, history) remains on your device until you delete it. **Uninstalling the Software leaves your local data in place by default.** You can remove it at any time with the data-management controls inside the app (see section 9), or by deleting the %LOCALAPPDATA%/VoiceWink folder.

The local items with their own shorter lifetimes are: a recording kept after a failed transcription, or after VoiceWink detected no speech in it (both removed when you retry, start a new recording, or close the app, with a 7-day sweep as backstop); recordings kept by the keep-recordings debugging option, unsent problem-report bundles, and prompt-trace logs (all swept after 7 days); and reference-image copies (kept while any History entry references them, then erased with the last of those entries). The memory-only capture buffer described in Section 2 is never written to disk at all.

Cloud-provider retention follows each provider's policy. Most providers offer zero-retention or short-retention modes; we encourage you to review the privacy policy of each provider you enable.

Lemon Squeezy and Stripe retain billing records as required by financial-services law.

Sentry retains crash reports for up to 90 days by default.

Cloudflare's logs for the update and model-download endpoints follow Cloudflare's standard retention; DV Studio keeps no update-check or model-download records of its own, only the daily and hourly totals described in Section 4.2, which it keeps for as long as it distributes the Software. The entries recorded for downloads of the versioned installer files, for the Microsoft Store channel's install reports and for the trial-start and website installer reports (Section 4.2) are kept for about three months on Cloudflare's systems, and the totals derived from them for as long as DV Studio distributes the Software. **Support correspondence.** Email exchanged with support@dvstudio.app (including any logs, screenshots, or diagnostic bundles you choose to attach) is retained for **90 days** by default after the conversation has closed, and longer only where the exchange relates to an open support ticket, a billing dispute, a security incident, or a legal/regulatory inquiry that requires us to keep the record. A report you build with "Report a problem" is prepared locally and attached to an email **you** send from your own mail client — the app never transmits it.
